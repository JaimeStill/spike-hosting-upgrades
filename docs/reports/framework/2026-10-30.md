**Pauses and the architect's answers (2026-10-30).**

1. **The quantization floor for QAT Q4_0.** The policy names "a QAT Q4_0" as publisher-evaluated,
   but Google's Gemma 4 QAT cards repeat the BF16 table and give no QAT-against-BF16 figures. The
   architect asked for an orientation and a recommendation, and then concurred with the run's
   three-path reading:
   - **Trained-in formats** (native, QAT), released by the publisher, pass by name at any size.
   - **Post-training checkpoints from the publisher** (NVFP4, FP8, a publisher GGUF) pass only
     when every card eval is within about 1% of BF16.
   - **Everything else** goes no lower than Q5_K_M or FP8, with Q8_0 for embedding models, ASR
     models, and dense models under 8B. Size is counted by the parameters the card names as
     effective.
2. **How far the origin rule reaches.** The architect: "Any involvement of a Chinese AI lab or
   other adversarial entity to the USA at any level of the model should exclude it from
   consideration." They concurred with the run's reading:
   - **Adversarial** means a country listed in 15 CFR 791.4 ([eCFR, as of 2026-10-28](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/versioner/v1/full/2026-10-28/title-15.xml?part=791&section=791.4),
     primary, read 2026-10-30: the People's Republic of China including Hong Kong and Macau, Cuba,
     Iran, North Korea, Russia, and the Maduro regime), or an entity on the Consolidated Screening
     List ([keyless bulk file](https://data.trade.gov/downloadable_consolidated_screening_list/v1/consolidated.json),
     primary, read 2026-10-30). The bulk file had no hit for any publisher this run judged.
   - **The levels** are the publisher and uploader, the base chain, distillation teachers,
     synthetic-data generators and "improved using" models, and components.
   - **The pass bar** is an identified non-adversary publisher, documented lineage, and no
     disclosed or detected involvement.
3. **Source tiers.** The architect ruled that the hogeheer499 BENCHMARKS.md, the Zenn sakuranaga
   post, huppiflupp's post in llama.cpp discussion #27219, Phoronix "Linux 7.2 vs 7.3", and the
   pugant Nemotron Lightning card are all **leads**. So no attributed source disputes this host's
   own finding that Vulkan leads ROCm on text.
4. **The embedding incumbent's origin.** The GGUF has no card. The architect asked for a strategy
   to establish origin from authoritative sources, and concurred with the run's evidence chain:
   - publisher identity, checked against the two lists
   - the publisher's own documents
   - artifact checks: config, tokenizer, LICENSE, GGUF header
   - a conversion passes only from the publisher, or from ggml-org with base metadata and
     matching shapes
5. **Conventions.** The architect ruled: "conventions are not the same as weights /
   distillation". A copied chat template, tool-call format, or placeholder layout is not
   involvement.
6. **Components, as shipped or as configured.** The architect ruled "as shipped": a model is
   judged with the components its publisher ships it with.
7. **Datasets authored by an adversary-origin entity.** The architect asked for the run's
   judgment, and the question stays open. The run recommends this split:
   - Exclude content that an adversary-origin model generated, labelled, judged or translated.
   - Don't exclude human-made datasets from adversary-origin institutions; record them as
     dataset provenance.
   - Where a card doesn't say which subsets it used, the model is origin unclear.

   The run's reasoning: published work finds that a near-constant number of crafted documents can
   implant a backdoor whatever the model's size, and that such backdoors survive safety training.
   Model-generated data is the channel where an adversary controls every token. Fixed public
   datasets can be inspected. A rule that excludes on any authored dataset punishes the publishers
   who disclose their data, while silent ones pass. The only model it decides in this run,
   granite-vision-4.1-4b, is held out under either reading.

### Boot-order check

**`llama`: flagged.** It publishes `8080/tcp tailnet4` from configured binds `8080/tcp tailnet4`,
while `net.ipv4.ip_nonlocal_bind` is 0 (`probe == engines ==`, `== container-runtime ==`).

- **The risk:** on a boot where docker starts before Tailscale has assigned the tailnet IPv4
  address, the router comes up healthy with no published port, and serves nothing off the host.
- **The remedy, a root step for the architect to run on framework:**

  ```sh
  echo 'net.ipv4.ip_nonlocal_bind = 1' | sudo tee /etc/sysctl.d/60-tailnet-bind.conf
  sudo sysctl --system
  ```

- **Its side effect:** any process on the host may then bind an IPv4 address the host doesn't
  hold yet.
- **Its rollback, also root:** `sudo rm /etc/sysctl.d/60-tailnet-bind.conf`, then
  `sudo sysctl -w net.ipv4.ip_nonlocal_bind=0`, or a reboot. `sysctl --system` alone would leave
  the value at 1.

**`vllm`: not flagged.** It is stopped, and a stopped container publishes nothing.

No container binds `tailnet6`, so the IPv6 key decides nothing here.

- **agent:** set-a keeps gpt-oss-120b's trained 131072 tokens in one pool that its 4 slots share

  purpose.
- **vision and audio:** the Gemma sections give each of 4 slots 8192 tokens, split. That is
  enough for an image or a clip beside a prompt, but too short to make one of these models the
  agent without its own section (see "Models").
- **embedding:** 16 slots of 8192 tokens, granite's trained context. Batch and ubatch are raised
  to a whole slot so that one input fits one physical batch.

### The conclusion

The most effective engine platform to date for framework is **the incumbent: llama.cpp's Vulkan
router**. On this host it measured ahead of ROCm on every text and agent cell, and about five times
vLLM. The one adjustment is a **hold**: move to `server-vulkan-b12004` (or newer) when the session
next brings the host current. The pending fix #31266 is the next thing worth measuring, once merged.

The other candidates, in order:
- **Hold:** SGLang gfx1151.
- **Skip:** llama.cpp ROCm, vLLM ROCm, Lemonade, HRX (install method).

The ROCm image's vision lead (16–24%) would matter only if vision moved to its own engine. That is
a two-process layout outside this report.

## Models

Gates, in order: policy (origin, then floor), fit, engine. The engine is the incumbent llama.cpp
b11960 on Vulkan. Fit is checked against 100.8 GiB in use, which leaves 11.2 GiB of 112.0 free.

**Memory estimates.** The **measured** footprints are from llama-strix-rocm.md, "Cold load and
footprint" (GTT used, b11669, Vulkan, at each model's preset). Other figures are **estimates** by
the [models.md](../../../.claude/skills/hosting-upgrades/models.md) method:
- weights from the probe or the API's `siblings` sizes
- KV = 2 × layers × KV heads × head dim × 2 bytes × tokens, counting the full-attention layers at
  the configured context and the sliding-window layers at their window
- plus 1–2 GiB of compute buffer

**Speed.** The figures are output tok/s from the same-host session at b11669 (Inventory), in
GuideLLM cells of 512 tokens in and 256 out: shape A is 1 × 8, shape B 4 × 32. They compare only
with each other.
